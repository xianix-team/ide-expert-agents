## Framework module: Lawfulness And Processing Principles

- **Module ID:** lawfulness-and-processing-principles
- **Applies when:** The system processes any personal data (module 00 found personal data).
- **Primary regime:** EU GDPR (mapped to ISO/IEC 27701 and NIST Privacy Framework)
- **Controls:** 7 (3 Automatable, 3 Partial, 1 Manual)

| # | Requirement (control question) | GDPR | Fine tier | Role | ISO/IEC 27701 | NIST PF | Severity | Automation | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Each processing purpose is documented and has a lawful basis (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests). | Art 5(1)(a)-(b); 6(1) | Upper (83(5)) | Controller | 7.2.1; 7.2.2 | ID.IM-P; GV.PO-P | High | Manual | Purpose and lawful-basis choice are legal. Note any purpose register, RoPA, or legitimate-interest assessment found in-repo. |
| 2 | Where consent is relied on, it is opt-in, specific per purpose, recorded (who, when, which notice version), and as easy to withdraw as to give. Processing is gated on it. | Art 4(11); 7(1)-(3) | Upper (83(5)) | Controller | 7.2.3; 7.2.4; 7.3.4 | CT.DM-P; CM.AW-P | High | Automatable | Inspect consent UI (no pre-ticked boxes), consent records (timestamp, purpose, version), withdrawal endpoint, and that optional processing (analytics, marketing, training) checks the consent flag. N/A if consent is not a basis. |
| 3 | Purpose limitation: personal data is not reused for incompatible purposes — in particular, user conversations and content are not fed into model training, fine-tuning, evals, or analytics without a basis. | Art 5(1)(b); 6(4) | Upper (83(5)) | Controller | 7.4.2 | CT.DM-P | High | Partial | Trace user content into training/fine-tune/eval datasets, feedback pipelines, analytics events, and provider "use data for training" settings. Compatibility of purposes is legal. |
| 4 | Data minimisation: only fields needed for the purpose are collected, stored, and passed onward — including into LLM prompts, tool calls, and agent memory. | Art 5(1)(c) | Upper (83(5)) | Both | 7.4.1; 7.4.4 | CT.DM-P; CT.DP-P | High | Automatable | Look for whole user/customer objects serialised into prompts, tool arguments, events, or third-party calls. Compare collected form fields with fields actually used. |
| 5 | Storage limitation: retention periods are defined and enforced for every store holding personal data (DB rows, logs, traces, conversation history, vector stores, caches, object storage, backups). | Art 5(1)(e) | Upper (83(5)) | Both | 7.4.7; 7.4.8 | CT.DM-P | High | Automatable | Inspect TTLs, lifecycle rules in IaC, scheduled purge/cleanup jobs, log-retention config, and backup expiry. Any store with no retention evidence is a gap. |
| 6 | Accuracy: personal data can be corrected, and AI-generated statements about identifiable people are not stored or acted on as fact without verification. | Art 5(1)(d) | Upper (83(5)) | Controller | 7.4.3 | CT.DM-P | Medium | Partial | Inspect update paths and whether model outputs about people (summaries, profiles, scores) are persisted, labelled as generated, or reviewed. Real-world accuracy needs evaluation. |
| 7 | Children: where an online service is offered directly to children, age is checked and parental consent is obtained below the national age threshold. | Art 8 | Lower (83(4)) | Controller | 7.2.3; 7.2.4 | GV.PO-P; CT.DM-P | Medium | Partial | Only if module 00 found children signals. Inspect age gate and parental-consent flow. The national age threshold (13–16) is legal. |
